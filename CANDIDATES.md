Bounty: https://make.fun/bounty/u-carbon-forge-snooze-button-roast-14cbafc6
Task: one line roasting an alarm clock ringing at 6 AM on a Saturday. Hard cap 200 chars; winning band from our past data 80–106 chars.

Scoring: 1–5 on each judge criterion, in order:
(1) clearly a one-line roast of an alarm clock at 6 AM Saturday;
(2) real laugh, unexpected turn, punchline pays off in the last few words;
(3) fresh angle — NOT "I hate mornings" / "who invented the alarm";
(4) tight, every word earns its place, sayable in one breath;
(5) sharp specific voice, concrete images not vague grumbling.
Total out of 25. Each candidate needs setup -> reframe -> kicker (two beats) and an original mechanism.

## Winner

**S2 (102 chars) — WINNER:**

> Saturday, 6 AM: my alarm clock is a tiny landlord evicting me from the only home I can afford — sleep.

## Why S2 won

- Joint-highest panel total (24/25), tied with S6 — and S2 wins the tie-break: smaller text (102 vs 104 chars), per the bounty tie-break rule.
- Strongest last-words payoff in the panel: the single final word "sleep" reframes the entire line — the "only home I can afford" is sleep, and the alarm is evicting the sleeper from it. That is the criterion-2 punch landing exactly where the judge looks.
- Two clean beats: (1) tiny landlord = the reframe, (2) eviction from the only affordable home = the escalation, with "sleep" as the kicker. No wasted words; every clause earns its place.
- Freshest concrete image among the top scorers: landlord/eviction is economic, specific, and visual (tiny landlord, eviction, home), not a generic mornings complaint. S6's bureaucrat is equally fresh, but its payoff ("sign it") is a verb phrase, where S2's is a single-word reveal.
- Inside the 80–106 winning band at 102 chars, and clearly a one-line roast of the 6 AM Saturday alarm (criterion 1: 5/5).
